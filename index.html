reo เร็ว · speaking speed

How fast is someone speaking? Open a recording or record yourself: reo counts the syllables and gives syllables per second of speech (พยางค์ต่อวินาที), utterance by utterance.

Or drop a file here. WAV (including μ-law and A-law phone recordings), MP3, M4A and more. Nothing is uploaded: the analysis runs on this device.

Speed over time

One bar per utterance (speech between pauses of half a second or more). Tap a bar to hear it and see its syllables; drag across the chart to measure any stretch, such as one part of a script. Grey ticks are utterances under 1 s of speech, too short for a rate.

Where to put the line is your call: have a few people rate some clips as too fast or fine, and set it between the two groups.

Utterance

Loudness in the 300–3300 Hz band; each dot is a counted syllable. Shaded: speech; gaps: pauses left out of the speaking time.

How it works and how accurate it is

reo finds each syllable as a peak of loudness in the 300–3300 Hz band that is voiced (the method of de Jong & Wempe, 2009, with the band and settings tested for Thai in korakot/pat), and divides the count by the time spent speaking. Pauses of 120 ms or more are left out, so the number is the articulation rate: how fast the words come when the person is talking, not how much they pause. The smoothing adapts to each utterance's own speed, so slow speech is not over-counted and fast speech is not under-counted.

Fast speech: exact up to about 6 syllables per second; above that some syllables merge (−5% at 6–7 /s, −12% above 7 /s), so very fast speech reads a little slower than it is, but still faster than slower speech.

Limits: one voice per channel. In a stereo call recording (agent on one side, customer on the other) each side is measured on its own; a mono recording of two people mixes their speeds. Music and loud noise add false syllables.
